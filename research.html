Performance Contracts for Noncontractible Recommendations

Working paper

with Jia Wei Zhang

How should a seller pay a recommender when sales are verifiable but the recommendation is noncontractible, and what can the contract guarantee when advice costs are private? We show that at most two publicly observed contracts with deterministic payments tied to sales yield the seller’s highest payoff in some equilibrium among finite menus. A behavior contract combines low cost promotion with truthful higher cost advice needed for credibility, while a truthful contract carries any remaining truthful service. Direct commitment avoids the resulting information and message rents, so its value is weakly higher. With state independent message costs, delegation attains the commitment value in equilibrium, but pooling limits its guarantee to no communication. We also show that strictly profitable campaigns require positive reach and an interior market composition along the maintained path, while uniform pricing transmits price effects beyond the audience.

performance contracts · limited commitment · strategic communication · recommendations · uniform pricing

Return Policy and Competition with Naive Consumers

Working paper

We study price and quality competition when some consumers mistake inferior products for superior ones and can return mistaken purchases. Returns make a seller’s net revenue from inferior quality hump-shaped in price, producing equilibria with either contiguous price supports or a gap between inferior- and superior-product prices. Whether sellers or consumers bear return costs determines quality provision and welfare.

Persuade before Evidence

Work in progress

with Mingzhao Dai and Jia Wei Zhang

We study how media power affects costly evidence acquisition in a disclosure game with competing senders. Public persuasion changes both receiver beliefs and incentives to produce hard evidence. Asymmetric media access lets an advantaged sender avoid acquisition, while symmetric access can reveal information at low costs but suppress evidence production when costs are high.
